01 · Core of the role

CRM data quality framework

How I'd own and maintain HubSpot data quality day to day: deduplication, record completeness, field accuracy, and ownership/assignment — across Contacts, Companies and Deals, plus the connected stack.

Mapped to JD “Maintain and improve the quality of commercial data in HubSpot — deduplication, completeness, accuracy and ownership.” (JD responsibility, paraphrased.)

CRM data-health composition

Illustrative split of the contact base by health state. Only ~41% is clean.

Prevention-first operating principles

Cleaning data once is a project; keeping it clean is the job. These six principles govern how I'd run HubSpot so the same issues don't come back next quarter.

Required at creation

  • Owner, and core fields, are required the moment a record is created — not patched later.
  • A blank required field should be impossible, not a clean-up task.

One system of record per field

  • Each field has exactly one owning system; everything else reads, never writes.
  • This is what stops Outreach and Planhat fighting HubSpot over lifecycle and stage.

Validate, don't trust

  • Property validation on email format, picklists and Amount > 0 catches errors at entry.
  • Free-text is the enemy of clean reporting; govern picklists hard.

Automate the catch

  • Every recurring error gets a saved list and, where it matters, a workflow alert to the owner.
  • The audit finds nothing new because the system already surfaced it.

Document before you build

  • No new property or workflow ships without a dictionary entry and a definition.
  • Undocumented automation is technical debt with a delay timer.

Measure the hygiene

  • A Data Health dashboard makes quality a number leadership can see trending.
  • What gets measured stops rotting.

Health snapshot by object & connected tool

Each object below carries its own severity summary. Expand for the issue, why it hurts the commercial teams, the exact HubSpot mechanism to fix it, and how to stop it recurring.

9.4% duplicate contacts Critical

In the illustrative sample, ~4,530 of 48,200 contacts are duplicates (same email or name+company). Reps log activity on the wrong record and marketing double-counts. Fix: run HubSpot's native Duplicate Management tool (Settings → Data Management → Duplicates) weekly, merge by most-recent-engagement, then deploy a workflow that normalises email to lowercase on create to stop re-entry.

27% of contacts have no owner Critical

~13,000 contacts sit unassigned, so no rep is accountable and they never enter a sequence. Fix: a round-robin workflow that assigns Contact owner on creation based on country/segment, plus a daily active list 'Contacts with no owner' as a safety net.

31% missing Job Title, 22% missing Country Medium

Segmentation and routing break when core firmographics are blank. Fix: make Country a required dropdown on forms, enrich via HubSpot's Breeze/inferred properties where licensed, and add a property-validation rule plus a 'completeness' active list for backfill.

41% stale (no activity >180 days) Medium

~19,700 contacts have had no engagement in six months, inflating the database and distorting marketing reach. Fix: a re-engagement workflow; if no open/click in 30 days, set Lifecycle to 'Other' and suppress, with an archival list reviewed monthly.

Inconsistent country & job-title formatting blocks segmentation Medium

Free-text entry has produced 'UK', 'U.K.', 'United Kingdom' and 'England' as separate values (illustrative). Lists and routing silently miss records. Fix: convert Country to a governed dropdown, run a one-off standardisation workflow to map legacy values, and lock free-text off going forward.

6.1% duplicate companies Critical

~760 of 12,400 companies are duplicates, usually from domain variants (acme.com vs www.acme.com). Account-level reporting and ABM lists fragment. Fix: Duplicate Management on Companies keyed on domain, plus a standardise-domain workflow that strips 'www.' and protocol on create.

28% missing Industry, 19% missing Domain Medium

Without domain, automatic association of new contacts to the right company fails. Fix: require Company domain on creation, enrich Industry from HubSpot firmographics, and run a weekly 'Companies missing domain' list.

14% of companies unassigned Medium

~1,740 accounts have no owner, so renewals and expansion have no DRI. Fix: assignment workflow tied to territory, and surface unowned named accounts to RevOps in a custom report.

Association integrity is largely intact Healthy

In the sample, ~92% of contacts are correctly associated to a company record, which is healthy. Maintain: keep the domain-match automation on and audit orphaned contacts (no company) weekly so it does not drift.

Parent/child company hierarchy missing for enterprise accounts Medium

Multi-market brands (the exact kind Storyteq sells to) are entered as flat, unrelated companies, so account-level revenue and whitespace are invisible. Fix: use HubSpot parent/child company associations for enterprise groups; maintain via a documented rule and surface group rollups in a custom report. (Depends on Storyteq's real account structure.)

24% of open deals have a close date in the past Critical

~140 open deals have a close date before today. A forecast built on this is fiction. Fix: a workflow that flags any open deal with Close Date < today to the owner and to a RevOps Slack channel, plus a required, validated Close Date at stage progression.

18% of deals have no owner Critical

Unowned deals never get worked and silently rot in pipeline. Fix: make Deal owner a required property at creation and add a daily 'Open deals, no owner' list routed to the sales manager.

39% of open deals have no Next Step Critical

Without a documented next step, deal reviews are guesswork. Fix: require a 'Next Step' text property and 'Next Step Date' to advance past Qualified; workflow alerts the owner when Next Step Date is overdue.

22% of deals stuck in stage >60 days Medium

~170 deals have not changed stage in two months, the classic signal of a stalled or dead deal still counted as pipeline. Fix: a 'days in stage' calculated property surfaced on a pipeline-hygiene dashboard; deals over threshold get a task to the owner to re-qualify or close-lost.

12% of deals have no Amount Medium

Coverage and weighted forecast cannot be computed on deals with a blank amount. Fix: require Amount > 0 with property validation before a deal can reach Proposal stage.

44% of deals are single-threaded Medium

Nearly half of open deals (illustrative) have only one associated contact — one champion away from slipping. Fix: a 'single-threaded open deal' active list and a stage-gate prompt to add a second stakeholder before Negotiation; track multi-threading as a pipeline-health metric.

Closed-won deals are well documented Healthy

In the sample, ~90% of closed-won deals carry an amount, source and at least one logged call — a clean basis for win analysis. Maintain: keep close-stage required fields enforced so the win dataset stays trustworthy.

Call activity not associated to ~17% of active deals Medium

Gong recordings exist but are not linked to the deal, so conversation intelligence is invisible in the deal record. Fix: verify the Gong–HubSpot integration matches on attendee email and deal association; audit 'active deals with zero logged calls' weekly as a sync-health check.

Owner mismatch between Gong and HubSpot Medium

When a rep's Gong user is not mapped to the same HubSpot owner, attribution drifts. Fix: reconcile the Gong↔HubSpot user mapping table during onboarding/offboarding; add it to the monthly integration audit.

Recording coverage on closed-won deals is strong Healthy

In the sample, ~88% of closed-won deals have at least one associated Gong call, which is healthy for coaching and win analysis. Maintain: keep the auto-association rule and spot-check monthly.

Gong deal-stage signals not feeding back into hygiene Medium

Gong can detect when a deal lacks next steps or a decision-maker on calls, but those signals don't reach HubSpot. Fix: where the integration allows, map Gong deal-warning signals to a HubSpot property and include them in the weekly anomaly review. (Depends on Storyteq's Gong license & integration scope.)

Sequence status not syncing to ~12% of contacts Medium

Contacts active in an Outreach sequence show no engagement in HubSpot, so marketing and SDR efforts collide. Fix: confirm the Outreach–HubSpot sync direction and field mapping for sequence state; add a weekly 'in-sequence but no HubSpot task' reconciliation.

Lifecycle stage drift between systems Medium

Outreach marks a prospect 'finished' while HubSpot still shows 'Lead', causing double outreach. Fix: designate HubSpot as system of record for Lifecycle; one-way map Outreach outcomes into a HubSpot 'Sequence Outcome' property, never overwriting Lifecycle directly.

Bounce/opt-out suppression is syncing correctly Healthy

Hard bounces and unsubscribes propagate back to HubSpot in the sample, protecting deliverability. Maintain: keep the suppression sync on and verify in the monthly audit.

Task and activity logging not consistently attributed Medium

Outreach calls and emails sometimes log against the wrong HubSpot owner or no owner, undercounting rep activity. Fix: verify the activity-sync owner mapping and reconcile 'activities with no owner' weekly so activity-based reporting and capacity planning stay accurate.

14% of accounts show a declining health score; 8% are red Critical

Planhat flags churn risk that never reaches the deal/renewal record in HubSpot, so Client Services and Sales are blind to it. Fix: sync Planhat Health Score into a HubSpot Company property and build a 'red-health renewal' active list that triggers a save-play task.

Renewal dates not consistently mirrored to HubSpot Medium

Renewal opportunities live only in Planhat for ~1 in 5 accounts, so renewal pipeline is understated. Fix: mirror renewal date and ARR into a HubSpot renewal pipeline; reconcile the two systems monthly.

Product-usage signals flow into account records Healthy

Core usage metrics sync into Planhat and summarise onto the account, giving CS a usable health view. Maintain: validate the field mapping quarterly so definitions do not silently change.

Onboarding milestones not visible to Sales for expansion timing Medium

Planhat tracks onboarding/adoption stage, but Sales can't see when an account is healthy enough to expand. Fix: sync a simple 'adoption stage' field to the HubSpot company and build an expansion-ready list, so upsell deals are created on evidence, not guesswork.

02 · Repeatable hygiene

Data audit cadence & connected-tool integrity

A repeatable routine the team can adopt on day one — across HubSpot, Gong, Outreach and Planhat — covering sync integrity, orphaned records, broken associations, stale deals and field drift.

Mapped to JD “Run regular data audits across HubSpot and connected tools (Gong, Outreach, Planhat) to keep systems aligned.” (JD responsibility, paraphrased.)

No documented audit cadence today Critical

In the illustrative current state there is no recurring audit, so issues are found only when a forecast looks wrong. Fix: adopt the daily/weekly/monthly checklist below, owned by RevOps, run from saved active lists and a Data Health dashboard.

Field drift between HubSpot and connected tools Medium

Lifecycle, owner and stage values diverge across HubSpot, Outreach and Planhat. Fix: define one system of record per field in the data dictionary and add a monthly cross-system reconciliation to the audit.

Broken associations accumulate silently Medium

Orphaned contacts (no company) and deals with no primary contact build up between releases. Fix: weekly saved lists for each orphan type, cleared to zero before the weekly pipeline review.

Audit is fully automatable from HubSpot's native tooling Healthy

Saved active lists, custom report types, the Duplicate Management tool and workflow alerts mean the entire cadence below can run from inside HubSpot with no external tooling. Maintain: keep each audit view saved and owned, so the routine survives staff changes and never depends on one person's memory.

The cadence, as a day-one checklist

Daily

  • Clear 'Open deals — no owner' list to zero.
  • Action 'Open deal, close date in the past' alerts.
  • Check overnight integration sync errors (Gong / Outreach / Planhat).
  • Resolve form-submission routing failures.

Weekly

  • Run Duplicate Management on Contacts & Companies; merge.
  • Review 'deals stuck in stage > threshold' and 'no next step'.
  • Clear orphaned records (contacts with no company; deals with no contact).
  • Refresh Data Health dashboard; log deltas before pipeline review.
  • Reconcile Outreach sequence state vs HubSpot tasks.

Monthly

  • Cross-system field-drift reconciliation (lifecycle, owner, stage).
  • Planhat health-score & renewal-date mirror check.
  • Gong & Outreach user↔owner mapping reconciliation.
  • Property-completeness trend; backfill campaigns where needed.
  • Governance review: new properties, naming, deprecations.

Anomaly checks & what each one signals

Past close dates inflate the current quarter Critical

24% of open deals (illustrative) carry a close date already in the past, overstating in-quarter pipeline. Check: 'open deal, close date < today'. Signal: forecast date hygiene is broken; rep has not re-set the date or should close-lost.

Deals stuck in stage too long Medium

22% of deals have not moved in >60 days. Check: 'days in current stage > stage threshold'. Signal: stalled deal still inflating weighted pipeline.

Missing Next Step Medium

39% of open deals lack a next step. Check: 'open deal, Next Step empty or Next Step Date overdue'. Signal: no path to close; commit confidence is unjustified.

Amount / stage mismatch Medium

Deals at Negotiation with €0 or a default amount distort coverage. Check: 'stage >= Proposal AND amount <= 0'. Signal: the number you are forecasting on is not real.

Single-threaded deals Medium

44% of deals (illustrative) have only one associated contact. Check: 'open deal, associated contacts = 1'. Signal: high slip/loss risk if the one champion goes dark.

Pipeline coverage is measurable once hygiene holds Healthy

At ~€16.3M open pipeline against a €6.0M quarter target the sample shows ~2.7x coverage — below the 3x+ rule of thumb but workable. Maintain: track coverage weekly on a custom report once the anomalies above are cleared, or the ratio is meaningless.

04 · Commercial analysis

Commercial business analysis (Excel-style)

The analyses RevOps runs to turn a clean CRM into commercial insight: stage-to-stage conversion, win rate, churn signals, pipeline coverage and source performance — with the pivot-table logic behind each.

Mapped to JD “Carry out commercial business analysis (Excel) to surface trends in conversion, win rate and pipeline.” (JD responsibility, paraphrased.)

Stage-to-stage conversion

Illustrative conversion rate between adjacent stages. The weak point is late-stage (Negotiation→Won).

The analyses & their logic

Win rate of ~24% is below where a clean funnel should land Medium

Closed-won / all closed = 24% (illustrative). Some of the 'loss' is data noise (dead deals never closed-lost). Analysis: COUNTIFS on Deal Stage = Closed Won vs Closed Lost, sliced by source and segment, after hygiene cleanup so the denominator is real.

Late-stage conversion is the weak link Medium

Negotiation→Won converts at 55% vs ~67% mid-funnel (illustrative). Analysis: stage-to-stage conversion as a pivot of deals entering vs advancing per stage; the drop localises coaching to negotiation/closing, not top of funnel.

Source performance is uneven and under-measured Medium

Original Source splits show paid vs partner vs outbound converting very differently, but 17% of deals have no clean source. Analysis: pivot of win rate and ACV by Original Source; first fix source completeness or the ROI read is wrong.

Churn signals are quantifiable from Planhat Healthy

Health-score bands map to renewal outcomes; red accounts (8%) concentrate churn. Analysis: cohort renewal rate by health band, joined Planhat→HubSpot, to forecast net revenue retention.

Coverage-vs-target is a clean weekly read once data holds Healthy

Open pipeline ÷ remaining target gives coverage; ~2.7x today (illustrative). Analysis: SUMIFS of open amount by close-quarter ÷ target, trended weekly to see if coverage is building or eroding.

Average sales cycle is long and uneven by segment Medium

Mean cycle from Discovery to Closed Won is ~78 days (illustrative) but enterprise deals skew far longer. Analysis: AVERAGEIFS of (close date − create date) by segment and source; the spread, not the mean, tells you where to set realistic close dates and stage thresholds.

ACV varies 3x across source — and you're under-investing in the best one Medium

Average contract value runs ~€23k overall but partner-sourced deals land materially higher (illustrative). Analysis: pivot of AVERAGE(Amount) and win rate by Original Source; combine to a simple expected-value-per-lead figure that should steer marketing spend.

Method note: in Excel these are COUNTIFS / SUMIFS over a deal export keyed on Deal Stage, Original Source and Close Date — equivalent to a HubSpot custom report. Every figure here is illustrative; on real data the same formulas apply unchanged.

No single data dictionary or naming convention Critical

Properties are created ad hoc with overlapping meanings ('Status' vs 'Lead Status' vs 'Stage'). Fix: a RevOps playbook with a governed data dictionary, naming convention and a change-control process before any new property ships.

Lifecycle and deal-stage definitions are not written down Medium

Reps interpret 'SQL' and 'Qualified' differently, so conversion metrics are not comparable. Fix: documented entry/exit criteria per lifecycle stage and per deal stage, enforced with required properties at progression.

Lead routing and ownership rules live in people's heads Medium

06 · Safe change

QA & workflow testing

How I'd QA and lightly test new automations and CRM workflows before they touch live data — so a well-intended workflow never mass-corrupts the records it was meant to help.

Mapped to JD “QA and test new CRM workflows and automations before they go live.” (JD responsibility, paraphrased.)

Automations are shipped straight to live data Critical

New workflows touch production records with no test pass, risking mass mis-assignment or bad overwrites. Fix: a documented test plan per workflow run against sandbox/test records first, with enrolment triggers verified before go-live.

No rollback thinking before deployment Medium

If a workflow misfires there is no defined undo. Fix: for each automation, document the rollback (re-enrolment off, property history revert, backup active list of affected records captured before launch).

HubSpot gives the building blocks for safe testing Healthy

Test mode on workflows, a sandbox environment, and property history all exist. Maintain: codify their use into a standard QA checklist so testing is routine, not optional.

Overlapping enrolment triggers cause silent double-processing Medium

Two workflows enrolling on similar criteria can both fire on the same record, e.g. assigning an owner twice or sending duplicate alerts. Fix: maintain a workflow register mapping triggers and actions; before launch, check for enrolment overlap and add suppression/exclusion lists where two automations could collide.

No post-launch monitoring window Medium

Workflows are switched on and forgotten, so a misfire is only caught when someone notices bad data downstream. Fix: for the first 48 hours after go-live, watch the enrolment count and spot-check affected records via property history; only then consider it stable and move on.

Standard pre-launch test plan (per workflow): 1) state the goal & success criteria; 2) build in a sandbox or against tagged test records; 3) dry-run enrolment triggers and check the audited count is what you expect; 4) verify each action on test records via property history; 5) capture a backup active list of records that would be affected; 6) define the rollback; 7) enable, monitor the first enrolments, then scale.

What I'd want from Storyteq in week one

To turn this illustrative plan into real execution, the dependencies on Storyteq's actual setup are short and concrete:

Access & visibility

  • HubSpot seat with admin or ops-admin permissions.
  • Read access to the Gong, Outreach and Planhat integration settings.
  • The current list of pipelines, deal stages and their intended exit criteria.

Context

  • The quarter's revenue target and segmentation, so coverage is measured against the real number.
  • Who currently owns routing, naming and property creation — the people, not just the process.
  • Any existing playbook or data dictionary, however partial.

Guardrails

  • A sandbox or test environment for workflow QA, or agreement to create tagged test records.
  • Sign-off on making core fields required, since it changes rep entry behaviour.
  • A standing 30 minutes in the weekly sales rhythm to review the Data Health dashboard.
Why this matters: the single biggest risk to a RevOps cleanup is doing it to the commercial teams instead of with them. Required-field rules and routing changes touch how every rep works daily — they land only if Sales, Marketing and Client Services see the dashboard improve and feel the forecast get more reliable. The plan above is sequenced so the teams feel the wins (cleaner pipeline reviews) before they feel the constraints (stricter data entry).

Where a recommendation would depend on Storyteq's real configuration, that dependency is stated in the text.

Methodology, in brief. The synthetic dataset was modelled on the data-quality profile typical of a fast-scaling B2B SaaS at ~170 people: a CRM that has grown faster than its governance. Severities were assigned by commercial impact — anything that distorts the forecast or leaves revenue unworked is Critical, anything that degrades reporting or efficiency is Medium, and anything functioning correctly is Healthy and flagged to be protected. The readiness score is a transparent weighted average of six area scores, with weights chosen to reflect that downstream metrics inherit upstream data errors. The maturity archetypes are illustrative stages, not benchmarks against any named company. On a live HubSpot instance, the same audit views, validation rules, workflows and analyses apply directly — only the numbers would change.

Built as a single self-contained HTML report with no external dependencies beyond web fonts. Traffic-light findings, the severity filter, the sortable maturity table and all six charts are interactive and theme-aware; the Download PDF button prints a clean A4 version with every accordion and section expanded for offline reading or sharing.
